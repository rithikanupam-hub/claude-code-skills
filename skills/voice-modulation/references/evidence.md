# Voice performance evidence — 2026-10-02

The working NotebookLM corpus is in `~/.claude/skills/voice-modulation/research/nlm/q1.json` through `q13.json` (notebook `e6aa9d3d-5b1a-4fd6-a6aa-dc2810188c1c`). These are synthesis notes. The primary sources below govern any research claim; observations from the project productions are local feedback, not general evidence.

| Practical inference | Verified evidence and limit |
|---|---|
| Leave processing room at meaningful boundaries. | [Winn et al., *Strategic Pauses Relieve Listeners from the Effort of Listening to Fast Speech*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10637151/) tested time-compressed narratives with pauses restored at salient boundaries. Recall recovered fully for normal-hearing young adults in the clear-speech condition; cochlear implant users showed a more modest 5–10% recovery after roughly a 20% loss. This is an experiment on compressed narratives, not an ideal pause-duration table for reels. |
| Do not strip all natural pauses in editing. | [Volín and Šturm, *The Absence of Pauses in Spoken Narratives and Memory Recall*](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/icphs-proceedings/ICPhS2023/full_papers/535.pdf) tested 24 listeners and eight audiobook excerpts. Removing pauses reduced overall missing-word recall (27.6% with pauses; 20.6% without). The authors call the effect statistically significant but not particularly robust; two of eight texts went the other way. |
| Use pitch contrast to mark meaning; avoid a forced monotone or exaggerated sing-song. | [Rodero, *Effectiveness, Attractiveness, and Emotional Response to Voice Pitch and Hand Gestures in Public Speaking*](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2022.869084/full) compared few, medium, and many pitch variations in public presentations with 120 participants. Medium variation scored highest for perceived effectiveness and attractiveness; results also depended on gesture and outcome measured. It does not supply a universal semitone target for narration. |
| Prefer a story-shaped delivery over a habitual news cadence when the film tells a story. | [Rodero and Cores-Sarría, *Best Prosody for News*](https://doi.org/10.1177/00936502211059360) compared broadcast and narrative reads of news. The narrative style increased physiological attention and arousal, while conscious adequacy/effectiveness ratings were similar. Style and pace changed together, so the study does not isolate an optimal WPM. |
| Brisk speech can sound engaged, but clarity and context determine the useful rate. | [Schirmer et al., *Angry, old, male – and trustworthy?*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0232431) found shorter duration among acoustic predictors of trustworthiness for brief acted sentences. That association is not evidence that every video should be accelerated. The fast-speech pause study above shows a comprehension cost when rate is pushed far enough. |
| Match TTS directions to the model and verify the audio. | [ElevenLabs' current prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices) says v3/v4 use audio tags, punctuation, and text structure for delivery; SSML break tags do not work there. [Google's Gemini TTS guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation) describes structured style metadata and inline vocal tags for current models. These are provider instructions, not guarantees that a take followed a cue or retained every word. |

## Findings deliberately kept out of the rules

- No universal WPM band. The NotebookLM answers combine advertising, news, lectures, audiobooks, and time-compressed laboratory speech; the tasks and listeners differ. Viral-video conversion claims in older local notes are not evidence that a voice should always run at 210–250 WPM.
- No universal pitch-range floor, “four emotions,” fixed pause length, or rule to delete every breath. The cited studies do not validate those as thresholds for a finished film. Choose the contrast that serves the sentence and check it by listening.
- No blanket instruction to suppress vocal fry, uptalk, or accents. First-impression studies are context-sensitive and can encode listener bias. Correct a vocal pattern when it obscures the intended meaning or when the performer wants it changed, not to enforce an identity stereotype.
- NotebookLM sometimes states an experimental result too broadly. Its “95% recovery” pause summary collapses listener groups and listening conditions; the primary paper distinguishes them. Treat numerical claims from the notebook as leads until checked at source.
