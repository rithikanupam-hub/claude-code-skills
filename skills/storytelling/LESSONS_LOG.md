# Storytelling lessons log (history)

The stories behind the rules. Not loaded by default. The rules now live in SKILL.md (shared), `types/<type>.md`, `voice.md` → "Rules from runs" and `research.md` → "Rules from runs". Moved here verbatim on 2026-11-06 by skill-improver.

- 2026-10-19 (rea-teardown, founder: "what is the need… to sell it's not gonna be worth because everyone clone this, you need to build a system that no one can clone"): an open 'what will you build?' ending was too soft for a founder audience. The payoff should take a stance the founder holds (here: features are copyable, systems aren't) and use their product as the example of that stance, not just as 'what I built'.
- 2026-10-19 (ich-bin-ein-berliner v5 film, founder: "I did not understand the story, what is it?"): a myth-busting film opened by denying a myth it never stated ("Berlin cheered, then laughed. Not at a doughnut.") and then ran 7 threads at the same speed. The cold listener (lost at line 8, "the doughnut was dropped after line 1") and the hook critic ("assumes the viewer knows the myth") both warned, and I fixed wording instead of order. For myth vs truth: state the myth plainly in line 1 ("You've heard X"), deny it in line 2, then explain in time order, with at most 3 supporting facts. A cold-listener "lost the thread" is a structure fix, never a wording fix. Before the voice, check the founder can retell the story in one breath.
- 2026-10-19 (rea-teardown): a 'their own docs say X' line passed two critics and the founder's ear, then failed at the browser drive: the live README never said it (FACTS had it [V/2] from secondary summaries). Before the voice step, open every quoted or paraphrased primary source live and copy the exact sentence into script.md; a 'docs say' line without a verbatim match is rewritten before recording, not after.
- 2026-10-19 (rea-teardown, founder: "check out the meet program"): when the founder claims first-hand use of a tool in the film ("I built X with REA"), check the codebase for a trace (deps, logs, dates) before scripting the link. Meet showed none (LiveKit, first build 30 Sep, before REA trended), so the script says "what I built" and the founder decides on the link. Two critic rounds both flagged the product pivot as the swipe point; keep the product beat short and concrete (the euros-to-dollars catch landed).
- 2026-10-07 (ai-acts-too-early, our own API test): the script critic caught a test confound I missed. The clips were spliced from separate TTS takes, and the model logged a new speaker per piece, which made it look cleaner than real speech. A one-take re-test reversed the result ("translated us perfectly" became "it reversed our booking"), and the founder approved the rebuild. For any first-hand test the film rests on, record the input as one continuous take and check the system's own logs (speaker ids, turn events) for artefacts of how the test was built, before scripting.
- 2026-10-06 (tokyo-voice-ruling v2, founder notes): (1) "The most informative fact must lead": the first lines say what happened in plain facts (a TikTok account made 188 videos in an AI voice commenters heard as Kenjiro Tsuda) before any chapter or background. Keep it true: never state a contested fact ("cloned his voice") as proven; attribute it ("commenters asked", "Tsuda said it imitated his own voice"). (2) Never "he sued": name the actor in every action line ("Tsuda sued"). Cold listeners twice inferred "AI clone" from softer lines, so the hedge must be spoken where the doubt matters ("The court never decided if the AI voice imitated Tsuda."). Cold-listener rounds also caught "line" heard as a boundary, "its owner" pointing at TikTok, and "symbolises" hard to catch by ear; documentary chapter titles spoken as "Chapter one: the copy." (bare "One. The copy." sounded like a fragment). Long legal stories overflow 80 s fast: move spans, filing dates and caveats to on-screen chips and keep the spoken line to the story.
- 2026-10-06 (tokyo-voice-ruling v1, founder: "not up to the mark… the other voice is not needed… it's a documentary, we do not have to ask the user whether… your voice is your job… make it more structured and easy to understand… strong challenge on the voice modulation"): a two-voice gimmick plus a Fichtean up-and-down made a legal story hard to follow. For a documentary: one narrator, time order with clear chapters, no viewer call-out or task line (story check item 10 does not apply to documentaries; end on a thought, not an instruction). Before the script, check the founder can retell the story in one breath; if they ask "is that the story?", the spine isn't clear enough yet.
- 2026-10-06 (voder-1939, founder: "simulated insta user"): after the cold-listener test, run a **simulated Instagram viewer**: a Sonnet subagent role-plays an ordinary 27-year-old scrolling Reels, reacts line by line (timestamp, stay/swipe, why), names the swipe second, the comment it would leave and what it would tell a friend. It found what critics missed: mechanism lines before the twist read as a 'science lecture' (swipe at ~20 s), so the twist moved from 32 s to ~20 s and the script fell from 202 to 148 words. Check its friend-summary for misreadings (it inferred 'secretly'). Say plainly it is a simulation, not audience data.
- 2026-10-05 (tokyo-voice-ruling, founder: "a voice war between a Japanese person and a US person accent"): the voice cast can carry the story. Here two narrators fight: a Japanese voice tells it, an American-accented "AI voice" (labelled) keeps cutting in and stealing lines, and the Japanese voice takes the story back at the ruling. Write the script as two parts (who says each line) from step 4, and plan hook takes for both voices. Never clone or imitate a real person's voice in a film about voice rights; keep accents respectful, with a native-speaker check.
- 2026-10-04 (customer-service-face v2, founder: "the thing that we had asked from her is a little complicated… give an easy example… where is this… how can I go to"): a demo question must be one any viewer understands on first listen, with no foreign names, places or jargon. "I'm in Prati. Where can I get pizza al taglio?" became "Hi! Where's the restaurant?" / "It's on the terrace, up the stairs to your left." Pick the everyday question a real customer asks first (where is…, how do I get to…, what time…).
- 2026-10-04 (customer-service-face v2, founder: "the girl is not moving until she's spoken to… the video closes for a certain time and it is very complicated"): in a product-demo film the demo is the proof, so it goes right after the hook (by 4–8 s), not after the explanation; cut the explanation to one "but/so" turn (one input → her), not a 5-part pipeline. Write in the beat table that the character is alive from frame 0 (idle loop) and the screen is never dark. Critics cut "You decide what she knows" and "She answers from what you give her": grounding is our setup, not in Google's post.
- 2026-10-04 (seo-mission-control-proof v5/v6, founder: "the script a little AI-ish… if you want to scale your content… you need to hear this… if you are not working like this you are way behind"): for a product/how-to film for marketers the founder wants a direct call-out voice: open by naming who it's for and the outcome they want, then the proof in numbers, then a numbered 'exact workflow' with practitioner terms (impressions, content gap, top three), and a 'you're falling behind' line. Smooth balanced lines ('Less typing. More deciding.', 'Here's the part…') read as AI. Keep the truth rules: 'you need to see this' alone is a hard fail (hides the subject), so put the proof in the same breath; universal claims ('how AI marketers do SEO') become a forecast or 'the exact workflow'; date windows end where the data ends.
