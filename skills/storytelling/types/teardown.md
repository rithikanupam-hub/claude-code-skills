# Type: Under-the-hood teardown

**Job:** an everyday action ("you turned your phone") opens onto the hidden mechanism behind it. The viewer understands *how* it works and feels smart.
**Examples:** `under-the-hood-slate-2026-10-19` (phone tilt, message journey, noise to image; the generic slate was rejected), `FINALS/rea-teardown`.
**Simulated viewer:** a curious non-engineer, and a builder on LinkedIn for tool teardowns.

## Spine
- The everyday entry point (something the viewer did today).
- The mechanism in 3–5 visible steps, in time order, one actor per line.
- The surprise inside the mechanism (the linked laptop, the tiny moving mass).
- Founder stance (tool teardowns only).

## Shape
Default frame: question → explanation → payoff (hooks-and-stories "Question → explanation → tool"). Every step is a visible event in the beat table (exploded object, detail camera move). Keep the mathematical state apart from the visible image. Bring the surprise forward: mechanism lines before any payoff read as a lecture, and that's where viewers swipe (voder).

## Ending and selling
- Topics must come from the founder's own work (founder 2026-10-19 rejected a generic slate). Check `REVISED_TOPICS.md`.
- Tool teardown for founders: end on a **stance the founder holds** ("features are copyable, systems aren't"), with our product as the example of that stance. Keep the product beat short and concrete. Critics flag a long product pivot as the swipe point.
- "Their docs say X" lines need a verbatim live match (research.md rules).

## Story check: extra items
- The cold listener can explain the mechanism step by step from the narration alone.
- **User first** when it's a how-to: who the user is, what they type, what they need, then a check of the output against that need.
- First-hand claims ("I built X with Y") have a trace in the codebase.
