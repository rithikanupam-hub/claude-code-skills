# Type: AI news story

**Job:** the viewer knows what happened, when, who did it, and why it matters to their work. Pillar "AI news and stories" (content-calendar).
**Examples:** `FINALS/agents-said-sorry`, `model-race`, `openai-nsw-bushfire` (parked).
**Simulated viewer:** a professional who uses AI at work and has seen the headline but not the detail.

## Spine
- The event: date, actor, what happened, all in plain facts in the first lines.
- Context: what was true before.
- Consequence: what changes for the viewer.
- Reported vs confirmed: kept apart, out loud.

## Shape
No head fake means no video: name what people assume about the event vs what is true. Length: 60–75 s (180–220 words) for YouTube/LinkedIn, 35–45 s for a reel. Add a smaller loop every 15–20 s.
Default frame: dopamine loop with **the news as the spine**; evergreen facts only support it. Use the "Fresh-news stories" method in `research.md` (primary-source chain, sources by role).
Two same-theme incidents can beat one (agents said sorry: OpenAI/Medicare + Claude Code). Score topic candidates in `topics.md` on freshness, discussion evidence, human character, quotable line, LinkedIn fit and Instagram fit (/30).

## Ending and selling
Never sell. End on what it means, or one thing to check in your own setup.

## Story check: extra items
- The event is ≤ 2 months old and dated on screen. This rule applies to news films only; every other type can use history and older data (founder, 2026-11-06).
- Shared rule 10 (no implied causes, no overstated paraphrases) is where news films fail most: agents said sorry ("a whole country", "wasn't public").
- Post-cutoff model names: judge clarity by the captions, and show the name on screen.

## Voice default
Brisk, clear newsroom narrator, about 180 wpm (the brisk prefix in `voice.md`).
