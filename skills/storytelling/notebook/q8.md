In **"How to Become a Storytelling Genius (Dopamine Ladders)"**, creator Kallaway breaks down the **Dopamine Ladder**, a six-level framework designed to trigger increasing releases of dopamine in a viewer's brain. Ascending a viewer through all six stages retains them until the end of the video and, over time, builds a **"Pavlovian effect"** where simply seeing your name or face triggers a dopamine hit.

Here is each step in order, including exact wording, core functions, visual/editing executions, and every example provided:

---

### **Level 1: Stimulation**
* **Exact Wording & Function:** The first 1 to 2 seconds of the video. It acts like **"a visual stun gun that gets someone to stop and lock in on one thing. It's like a deer in the woods noticing something out of the corner of their eye and they can't resist the urge but to pick up their head look and lock in on that one spot"**. Dopamine comes from rapid, subconscious ("bottoms-up") processing in 200 milliseconds, picking up vivid color, motion, brightness, and contrast before the brain comprehends meaning.
* **Editing & Visual Mapping:** Use high-contrast colors, brightness, physical movement, and a custom visual aesthetic/color palette to cut through the feed.
* **Examples Given:**
  * A test showing 9x16 blurred images where eyes automatically track the options with high contrast, motion, and color.
  * Social media in grayscale/black-and-white (which loses viewer interest due to lack of visual contrast).
  * An attractive person standing in front of a vivid red-and-orange moving graphic.
  * *Citizen video walkthrough:* A slight camera zoom-in showing two people hugging, shot from a unique car perspective.

---

### **Level 2: Captivation**
* **Exact Wording & Function:** Known as **"the curiosity stage."** Kallaway states: **"Massive amounts of dopamine are released in the viewer's brain as soon as they are captivated by an open question that they ask in their own head. Many people call this a curiosity loop"**. Because the human brain evolved as a **"problem-solving machine,"** opening a non-obvious, relevant question triggers curiosity.
* **Editing & Visual Mapping:** Display text overlays, show intriguing visual details, or state a rhetorical question in text/audio to implant a question in the viewer's mind.
* **Examples Given:**
  * Posing the question: *"I wonder when the aliens are visiting Earth"*.
  * A makeup hook (*"Here's how you do your makeup for the holidays"*), which fails to captivate viewers who do not care about makeup because the question lacks relevance.
  * *Citizen video walkthrough:* On-screen text reading *"Bro what did I just witness?"* combined with a shot of a shoelace caught in a car door, prompting questions like *"What are we about to see?"* and *"Is he aware?"*.

---

### **Level 3: Anticipation**
* **Exact Wording & Function:** **"Anticipation is what happens when a viewer begins to guess in their head what the answer might be to the question you just posed in level two. This is essentially a mystery novel playing out in their head in real time as they watch the video"**. The highest dopamine release occurs right before the answer is revealed. Kallaway refers to this as **"the edging of the storytelling world"**.
* **Editing & Visual Mapping:** Keep facts clear so the viewer can make logical guesses. Ratchet up tension using misdirection, non-obvious twists, and **"head fakes"** that reset or prolong the curiosity loop.
* **Examples Given:**
  * A visual hook showing a car slowly approaching a person, causing the viewer to anticipate whether they will get hit.
  * *Citizen video walkthrough:* As the car pulls away and the character falls backward, the viewer anticipates whether he will be dragged down the street.

---

### **Level 4: Validation**
* **Exact Wording & Function:** **"Validation comes when you do end up giving them the answer to the question that you popped in level two. In other words, level four is you closing the loop"**. The goal is to reward the viewer with a non-obvious answer or resolution:
  * **Entertainment:** **"resolution of the story you don't end it on a cliffhanger you actually wrap up the loose ends and close the loop nicely"**.
  * **Education:** **"the validation or reward in this case would be you actually sharing the tip or the story or the recommendation that gives them concrete value that they can use to solve a problem"**.
* **Editing & Visual Mapping:** Deliver the payoff clearly using editing techniques like hard cuts, match cuts, or showing the resolution.
* **Examples Given:**
  * The approaching car/truck visual hook resolves safely, closing the loop and releasing dopamine.
  * *Citizen video walkthrough:* A hard match-cut transitions from him falling to him being dragged down the street while singing his song—closing the safety question while delivering a surprising, non-obvious payoff.

---

### **Level 5: Affection**
* **Exact Wording & Function:** **"Affection is the stage where the viewer starts taking a liking to the creator or personality that was delivering the story and this is why it's kind of hard for Faceless content to fully ascend to that Pavlovian peak"**. Levels 1–4 focus on the *message* (the video itself), whereas Levels 5–6 transition to the *messenger*. When viewers like and trust the creator, dopamine releases upon seeing them, giving the creator a **"longer leash"** to hold attention.
* **Editing & Visual Mapping (4 Tactics for Affection):**
  1. **Attractiveness:** Show yourself on camera if you are good-looking.
  2. **Vibe:** Mind your posture, attire, and carriage.
  3. **Smiling:** Joy transfers through the camera lens.
  4. **Solving a Problem (Value):** The strongest driver for education; helping someone solve a problem builds maximum trust regardless of appearance.
* **Examples Given:**
  * *Citizen video walkthrough:* Finding the stunt funny/shocking creates a "hat tip" reaction, making the viewer willing to watch his next video to see what stunt comes next.

---

### **Level 6: Revelation**
* **Exact Wording & Function:** **"Revelation... is really where the viewer begins to realize that you're going to be a continued source of value for them over time"**. Hitting Revelation establishes the **Pavlovian response**: **"it means if they hear your name see your face or see your name even without watching the content they're going to get the dopamine hit that leads them to watch it"**.
* **Editing & Visual Mapping:** Consistently deliver non-obvious, differentiated value across multiple videos.
* **Examples Given:**
  * **MrBeast:** Viewers know seeing his face guarantees maximum entertainment, achieving a Pavlovian response.
  * **Dating Analogy:** Consistently feeling love and positive emotion around someone causes you to get a hit of dopamine whenever you hear their name in the future.
  * *Citizen video walkthrough:* Realizing over several videos that the creator consistently combines viral stunt marketing with music, leading viewers to associate his face with an entertaining music drop.
