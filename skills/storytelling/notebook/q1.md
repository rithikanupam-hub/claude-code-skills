The **five-line story blueprint** presented by filmmaker Tim Runia is a framework designed to capture the "emotional core" or "life thread" of a video before getting lost in technical details, gear, or complex editing.

---

### Core Purpose of the Blueprint

Tim Runia developed this method after struggling with overthinking, endlessly rewriting outlines, or staring at messy editing timelines without knowing what the video was truly about. He emphasizes that **"in this chaos of decision making... You're losing the core of the story. And this is a big issue, because the core of your story is one of the most important things. Because if you don't know what the story is really about, no editing trick or no camera trick is going to save you from that"**. The 5-line method acts as a simple blueprint to establish the story's heart upfront.

---

### The Five Lines: What Each Line Must Do

1. **Situation (The Starting Point)**
   * **What it does:** Establishes the setting, context, background, and characters so the audience understands the starting environment.
   * **Speaker's exact wording:** *"The starting point. Where are we and what's happening. And how does the world look like where we're in"*. It *"gives the viewer a clear starting point of where we are, what's happening and who's in the story. They will know the world that we're in and what we're actually talking about"*.

2. **Desire (The Goal or Need)**
   * **What it does:** Defines what the main character wants to achieve or obtain, which sparks curiosity and gets the audience emotionally invested.
   * **Speaker's exact wording:** *"So what does the character want? What does she want to obtain, or what does she want to reach?"*. This line *"shows the goal or the needs that's driving the story. And this usually really sparks the curiosity of the audience and this is the part where people will lean more into the story, and they start to care about whether you're getting the thing or not"*.

3. **Conflict (The Tension or Obstacle)**
   * **What it does:** Introduces the challenge, obstacle, or tension standing between the character and their desire.
   * **Speaker's exact wording:** *"What gets in the way? Where's is the tension or what is an obstacle that makes it hard to reach the desire?"*. This *"introduces tension or an obstacle. And this is really something that stories need. Because if there's no tension, if there's no obstacle, if there's no challenge, there's not really a reason to keep watching for the audience"*.

4. **Change (The Turning Point)**
   * **What it does:** Represents a shift, realization, insight, or action that changes the character's perspective or situation and drives forward momentum.
   * **Speaker's exact wording:** *"So what happens that shifts something This could be a turning point in the story. Something big or something small or just a different way of seeing something"*. It *"marks a turning point in the story. So it could be an insight or an action or anything that shifts everything... It really shows growth and it gives the audience the feeling of movement forward"*.

5. **Result (The New Reality)**
   * **What it does:** Concludes the story, shows how the character or world has changed compared to the start, and provides closure.
   * **Speaker's exact wording:** *"So how does it end? What's the new reality and how is the situation different now from when we started"*. It *"should really give your audience satisfying takeaway a feeling of closure, an ending, and it should be something that ties everything of the whole story together. And it shows how the world or the situation or the character is different from the starting point"*.

---

### Common Mistakes and Pitfalls

* **Mistaking the 5 lines for the entire video:** A primary misconception is expecting these five lines to represent the final, detailed script. Tim clarifies: **"these five lines aren't your whole video. They are some sort of launchpad... You can use them to kick off your story and start building from your five lines, or you can use them to come back at any time when your project is overwhelming you"**.
* **Getting distracted by technical production over story structure:** Overthinking camera gear, complex visual transitions, voiceovers, or color grading before securing the core story logic.
* **Omitting conflict or tension:** Creating a narrative without real obstacles, which leaves the audience with no compelling reason to stay engaged.
* **Planning paralysis:** Trying to detail every scene from the beginning rather than capturing the concise 5-line foundation first.

### Examples Provided in the Source

#### Example 1: Hollywood Feature (*The Lion King*)
Tim demonstrates how even massive film plots fit this exact structure:
* **Situation:** *"Simba is a young prince excited to one day rule the Pride Lands."*
* **Desire:** *"He wants to prove he's brave and worthy of being a king."*
* **Conflict:** *"After his father's death, Simba runs away, ashamed and afraid to face his past."*
* **Change:** *"Simba grows up and with help from his friends and a vision from his father, Simba returns to confront scar and take his place as a king."*
* **Result:** *"He restores balance to the Pride Lands and steps fully into who he was meant to be."*
*(Note: Tim points out that while individual subplots are omitted, "it is the whole story".)*

#### Example 2: Tim Runia’s First YouTube Video (5-Line Blueprint)
Tim breaks down his own 8-minute YouTube video into five core lines:
* **Situation:** *"I'm a filmmaker from the Netherlands and I've loved making videos since I was a kid."*
* **Desire:** *"My dream was to be a YouTuber and sharing stories from the heart, not just for clients."*
* **Conflict:** *"But every time I tried to start, self-doubt and overthinking pulled me back."*
* **Change:** *"When I let go of the YouTube dream, I finally realized I was focusing on the wrong reasons and I just wanted to create stories."*
* **Result:** *"So I finally sat down and started, and this video is the result, and that first step pushed me to go all in on this YouTube journey."*

#### Example 3: Expanding the Blueprint (10-Line Version)
Tim illustrates how the 5-line foundation can be expanded into 10 lines by adding detail without altering the core framework:
* **Situation:** *"I'm a filmmaker from the Netherlands and I've loved making videos since I was a kid."*
* **Desire:** *"My dream was to be a YouTuber and sharing stories from the heart, not just for clients."*
* **Conflict (Expanded):** *"But every time I tried to start, self-doubt and overthinking pulled me back. My mind kept yelling, you can't go for making ads for big companies to making YouTube videos. Those fears became excuses: I'm too late and too old, I don't have enough skills. Gear. Security."*
* **Change (Expanded):** *"So I let go of the big YouTube dream and at first it felt good and I felt free. But then the same question kept coming back: why did I actually want to do YouTube all the time? When I stripped away views, subscribers and money, I realized it's about the joy of creating and sharing stories."*
* **Result (Expanded):** *"That realization made me push through self-doubt, focus on the process and the video you're watching right now is the result of that. It wasn't easy, but I already learned so much, and it even made me go all in on YouTube and share every lesson along the way."*

Tim concludes: **"going from five lines to ten lines doesn't change the core of the story. It just adds more richness and more details"**.
