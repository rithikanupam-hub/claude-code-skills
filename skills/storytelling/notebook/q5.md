### 1. "How To Become A Master Storyteller" (Kallaway)

* **Pacing & Script Rhythm:**
  * **The "But & Therefore" Rule:** Connect story beats using the words **"but"** (complications) and **"therefore"** (actions/consequences) instead of "and then". "And then" creates a boring list of events, whereas "but" opens conflict loops in the brain and "therefore" closes them.
  * **Sentence Length Variety:** Alternate short, medium, and long sentences to establish a natural cadence that prevents predictable, monotonous pacing.
  * **The "Jagged Edge" Script Formatting Check:** Place every sentence on a new line in your script document. Look down the right margin: a **jagged edge** indicates healthy sentence length variation, while an even margin alerts you to flat, monotonous pacing.
* **Hooks & Openings:**
  * **Punchy Plot Setup:** Make the first line directly indicative of the plot. Avoid vague, opaque openers like "wait till you see this".
  * **Show While You Tell:** Pair audio hooks with an immediate **visual hook** on screen, as human eyes process information faster than ears.
* **Angle & Structure:**
  * **Apply a "Story Lens":** Frame common topics using a unique angle or specific prism (e.g., focusing on business impact rather than surface-level gossip) to stand out.
* **Endings, Payoffs & Retention:**
  * **Write the Ending First ("The Last Dab"):** Identify your final line before drafting the middle. Craft the last line to be so memorable that viewers would share the video if that's all they heard.
  * **Seamless Looping:** Ensure the final line acts as a natural setup for the video's opening line when short-form content replays.
  * **Reverse Scripting Method:** Write the first and last lines first, leave space in between, and fill in the middle conflict beats.
* **Tone & Delivery:**
  * **The One-Friend Protocol:** Script content as if typing a text message or recording a voice note to a single close friend. Tape a picture of that friend directly under the camera lens during filming to maintain direct 1-on-1 intimacy.

### 2. "The Psychology of Killer Hooks" (Kallaway)

* **Hook Foundations & Interrupt Theory:**
  * **Trigger the "Freeze Point":** Force viewers to stop scrolling by answering two implicit mental questions in the first seconds: **"What is this about?" (Subject)** and **"What will I get if I keep watching?" (Stakes/Outcome)**.
  * **The 4 S's Checklist:**
    1. **Subject:** Instantly identify the core subject (person, place, object, or goal).
    2. **Stakes:** Establish a clear reason to care (curiosity scenario or desired outcome/pain point).
    3. **Speed (Compression):** Deliver the subject and stakes in as few words as possible without speaking too fast (respect the brain's limited working memory).
    4. **Super Clear:** Use simple words with zero wordplay or ambiguous setups.
* **Triple Hook Alignment:**
  * **Align All 3 Layers:** Ensure the **Text Hook** (on-screen text), **Spoken Hook** (spoken dialogue), and **Visual Hook** (visuals) reinforce the exact same message. Misalignment creates cognitive confusion and causes scrolling.
* **Text Hook Rules:**
  * **Substance Formula:** Combine a **Subject Repeater** (capitalized jargon or proper noun) with a **Pain Point Reminder**, **Desired Outcome Teaser**, or **Future State Change**.
  * **Extreme Specificity:** Use exact numbers and dollars (\$2,700 or 22 days) instead of vague generalities.
  * **Power Phrases & Remixing:** Incorporate 1–3 word attention-grabbing phrases (e.g., "scientifically impossible to skip") and twist existing power phrases to create fresh variations.
  * **2-Line Limit & Phrasing Breaks:** Cap system text hooks at **2 lines maximum**, breaking lines where natural phrasing occurs.
  * **No Punctuation or Transitions:** Exclude all punctuation (except quotes/parens) and omit transition words (*and, but, or, therefore*) in title text.
  * **3-Second On-Screen Duration:** Keep text hooks on screen for at least **3 seconds** to allow full visual and reading processing.
* **Retention & The "Lock-In Zone" (First 5–10 Seconds):**
  * **Confirm the Hook Claim:** Validate the hook in the 2–4 sentences immediately following it to prove you are not bait-and-switching the viewer.
  * **Provide a Trust Anchor:** Present concrete proof (data screenshots, studies, or case studies) within the first 10 seconds to pass rapid viewer trust evaluations.
  * **Production Packaging:** Maintain clean framing, good lighting, and crisp audio to signal effort and build trust.

### 3. "If you learn one skill in 2026, make it storytelling" (Storyblocks)

* **Pre-Writing & Core Purpose:**
  * **Define the Target Feeling First:** Before writing a single line of script, write **one sentence at the top of your document** specifying the exact emotion you want the viewer to feel at the end.
* **Hooks & Opening Lines:**
  * **Start at the Precipice:** Begin the script at the moment right before everything changes, skipping lengthy backstory setups.
  * **Conversational Hook Formulas:** Use opening lines you would naturally say to a friend in real life.
  * **The 4-Part Specificity Rule:**
    * Replace vague time frames with **exact time**.
    * Replace general results with **concrete numbers**.
    * Replace broad actions with **physical details**.
    * Describe what the emotion **physically felt like** rather than just naming the feeling.
* **Narrative Structure & Payoffs:**
  * **Relatable Turning Points:** Focus on ordinary, everyday moments where a shift occurs rather than waiting for extreme life crises.
  * **Authentic Lessons:** Share real realizations, even if incomplete or still unfolding.
  * **The "Give Back" Payoff:** End scripts by giving something back to the viewer (e.g., validating them or offering genuine value) rather than making selfish demands for engagement.
* **Visual Scripting & Editing Guidelines:**
  * **Two-Column Shot List:** Plan scripts in two parallel columns: **Script text line-by-line** on the left and **Visual ideas / reference images** on the right.
  * **Select Music First:** Choose background audio before editing video to lock in emotional pacing.
  * **Edit in Script Order:** Cut footage following the planned shot list order rather than assembling clips randomly.
  * **Minimalist Transitions:** Default to hard cuts; save flash transitions or overlays (light leaks, film burns) strictly for major narrative shifts.
  * **Pacing Room:** Allow shots extra seconds to breathe so viewers have time to absorb emotional beats.

### 4. "The ONLY 5 Lines You Need To Tell Any Story" (Tim Runia)

* **The 5-Line Blueprint Method:**
  * Map out the core emotional spine of the video using 5 foundational lines before drafting dialogue:
    1. **Situation:** Define the starting point, setting, and baseline reality.
    2. **Desire:** State what the character/creator wants to reach or obtain (sparks curiosity and audience investment).
    3. **Conflict:** Introduce the obstacle or tension getting in the way (essential for retention).
    4. **Change:** Highlight the turning point, action, or shift in perspective that drives forward movement.
    5. **Result:** Deliver the final outcome, new reality, and satisfying takeaway.
* **Expansion & Script Verification:**
  * **The 5-to-10 Line Expansion Rule:** Expand the 5 core lines into 10 lines by adding specific internal struggles, excuses, or details while preserving the 5-stage backbone.
  * **Structural Checkpoint:** Refer back to the core 5 lines whenever feeling overwhelmed during writing or editing to ensure every scene connects to the primary narrative thread.
