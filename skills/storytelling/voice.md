# Voice options (checked 2026-09-30)

- **Soniox** does speech-to-text only; it can't narrate. Use it, or `gemini-3.5-transcribe`, for the word timestamps (`words.json`). The key is in `lingosync/.env`.

- **Fish Audio** (`s2.1-pro`, about $15 per 1M chars). Use it for accents and character voices that Gemini lacks (e.g. Japanese-accented English). Runs on `FISH_AUDIO_API_KEY` in `.env`. Added 2026-10-06, not yet tested on a film.
- **fal.ai TTS** (ElevenLabs and MiniMax speech are hosted there; confirm the current model IDs on fal). These run on the `FAL_KEY` in `.env`. Use them as the comparison take.
- **Own voice / Qwen clone** (our app). Use it only when the story is about the project speaking in someone's voice.
- **Music:** Lyria (`lyria-3-pro-preview`, `lyria-3.5`) runs on the same Gemini key. Ask for the tempo and mood from the script's voice direction, keep it under the voice and let it end hard on the last frame.

## Directed narration recipe (2026-10-01, Georgetown v7; founder: "make sure the voice modulation is proper and it lands the dialogue")
1. One whole-script take with emotion cues does NOT change mood between chapters (measured: same pace and pitch spread everywhere; the "serious, slower" turn was the fastest part). Record **each chapter separately** with ElevenLabs v3 (same voice, `stability` 0.35), with its own lead cue (`[intrigued]`, `[excited]`, `[serious] [slowly]`, `[matter-of-fact]`, `[warmly] [slowly]`), 2 takes per chapter.
2. Measure each take (`motion-director/scripts/voice_check.py`): words vs script (Soniox), wpm, pitch median and spread. For a question, measure the last word's **peak against the preceding 4 words** (a single word's own slope misleads). Keep the takes that match the score.
3. Yes/no questions phrased as "to check: is X working?" fall in every take; a direct "one question: does X work?" lifted.
4. Join chapters at the transition pauses (0.8–1.1 s), each chapter loudness-matched first; then edit only silences with `scripts/voice_edit.py` (hook gaps ≤0.35 s, other gaps ≤0.55 s, beats before reveals 0.5–0.75 s). No speed or pitch change.
5. Second opinion: Gemini 3.1 Pro listening review per line (`gemini_voice_review_*.md`). It confirmed the chapter changes became audible (v7) where the single take had none; treat its intonation claims with care when they contradict the pitch measurement. The founder's ear decides.

## Comedy / brand-film modulation recipe (2026-10-02, same-call) — SUPERSEDED for funny films by "Funny short-form modulation (v2)" below
Founder on the result: "the voice does not really keep the vibe… it is now slow". Keep steps 3–7 (sequential TTS, Soniox check, energy-measured pauses, silence-only edits, cues.json); replace the deadpan delivery rules in step 1.
Use for short narrated brand films and funny TOFU films (deadpan narrator over pictures and other voices). Example: `marketing/video/FINALS/same-call/recipe/story/voice_score.md`.
1. **Write a voice score first**, one row per line: mood, stress word (one per line), pause plan in seconds. Rules: setup at 160–175 wpm → a 0.35–0.6 s beat → the punch slower (≈130–145 wpm), lower and flat, falling. Under-sell every joke; warmth only on the brand line. One-word beats ("Every. Single. Call.") staccato, each word lower than the last. A question that sets up a payoff rises, then a 0.4 s beat. The narrator never talks over a client line or the app's speech; stop 0.3 s before another voice.
2. **Record each line separately** (Gemini `gemini-2.5-pro-preview-tts`, voice Charon), with the line's own direction as the style prefix ending "Say only this: ". Put `...` in the text where the score wants the beat. 2 takes per narrator line; other voices 1 take (clients in their own language with a native-speaker style prompt, Orus for Italian man, Kore for Japanese woman; Puck for a small comic character).
3. **Send TTS requests one at a time**: parallel calls hit 429 on the preview TTS model (the per-minute quota); retry with a 25 s wait.
4. **Check every take** with Soniox (`words_soniox.py <wav> <out> [lang]`, language hint now a third argument): all words present, no style text read aloud, question punctuation right (Soniox writes "?" or "." from the intonation, a fast way to catch a falling question; N4 take a came back "colleague." and was rejected).
5. **Measure pauses from audio energy, not from word timings.** Soniox word gaps of 0.2–1.1 s were mostly word tails at −19 to −29 dB; only gaps below about −38 dB from the take's peak are real silence. Pitch: compare the stressed/question word's peak with the words before it (librosa pyin): N4 "colleague?" 132 Hz vs ~85 Hz before = a real rise.
6. **Edit silences only** to the score (cut in the middle of a measured silence, keep 0.04 s lead and 0.14 s tail, 10 ms fades). No speed or pitch change. Took 0.59–0.97 s beats down to 0.35–0.5 s.
7. **The voices are the timeline:** place every clip in `cues.json` (start, duration, word times); the film reads `cues.js`, captions light on the word times, and the mix reads the same file.

## Funny short-form modulation (v2) — researched 2026-10-02
**Authority (2026-10-02):** the founder's `marketing/skills/voice-modulation/SKILL.md` governs performance. Where it differs from the numbers below, it wins: WPM and pitch range are measurements of a take, not targets; no mechanical maximum gap and never remove every pause; no tempo/pitch changes to manufacture energy; direct each line with its intention and context. Keep the measurements below as diagnostics only.
 (founder: "the voice does not keep the vibe… it is now slow… research how modulation should be")
Research: NotebookLM notebook "VO modulation for funny short-form video" `2be822f7-5cde-402c-95b6-131e81af0a06` (VidMob TikTok study via Social Media Today; Rodero 2022, Frontiers in Communication, pitch variation; ElevenLabs v3 prompting guide; VoiceBros short-form VO guide; r/VoiceWork on deadpan; rule-of-three).
Why the deadpan v1 failed (measured): 1.5 words/s, pitch p5–p95 10 st, beats of 0.35–0.5 s between words, ordinary lines read flat.
Rules:
1. **Pace:** setups at 3.5–4.2 words/s (210–250 wpm). VidMob: talk tracks at ≥4 words/s converted 19% better than 2–3 words/s. Creators commonly speed narration 1.05–1.10× in post; stay within that (founder heard 1.12–1.15× as "manipulated").
2. **Silence:** cut breaths and gaps (no inner silence over 0.3 s) EXCEPT one 0.25–0.4 s beat right before each punchline. The punch changes pitch or tone; it does not slow down much (≥170 wpm).
3. **Pitch:** aim for a medium-to-wide range. Rodero 2022: monotone (~20 st range) lowered arousal ("sensory adaptation"); medium variation (~24 st) rated most effective; wide (~29 st) the happiest. Our measure (p5–p95, `voice_vibe.py`) should be ≥15 st; v1 Charon deadpan measured 10 st, v2 lively voices 15–23 st.
4. **Emotion:** smile in the voice by default; ≥4 distinct emotions across a film (VidMob: 4+ emotions → 3.3× conversion). Deadpan only when the LINE is absurd ("the more deadpan the voice, the more absurd the line should be"); otherwise it reads slow.
5. **Write the joke into the line:** rule of three, the third item is the punch and carries the stress ("Italian on line one. Japanese on line two. Lunch? … Never heard of it.").
6. **Engine (default since 2026-10-02): Puck on `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` via the Directed Gemini recipe below**; the founder chose it by ear over two Fenrir takes ("puck is the best"). Older notes: ElevenLabs v3 on fal (tags `[mischievously] [excited] [sarcastic] [laughs]`, CAPS for stress, `…` for the beat; no SSML breaks) when fal is unlocked. When fal is locked (TOP_UP), Gemini `gemini-2.5-pro-preview-tts` with a base persona ("witty, fast-talking social-media narrator with a smile in your voice… about four words per second, lots of pitch movement, no long pauses") plus a per-line direction. Lively Gemini voices: Fenrir (Excitable), Sadachbia (Lively), Puck (Upbeat). Charon is a documentary voice; don't use it for comedy.
7. **Measure before the founder listens:** `motion-director/scripts/voice_vibe.py` (words/s, longest gap, gaps > 0.3 s, pitch range, pitch movement, transcript). Reject takes that drop words (Sadachbia once read "Never heard of.") or lose the question beat (Puck read "Lunch never heard of it" with no "?").
8. The founder picks the voice by ear from an 8 s hook sample per voice, level-matched to −16 LUFS, before the full narration is recorded.
9. **Clarity beats speed** (founder 2026-10-02 on the v2 sample: "It says Italian online 1, Japanese online 2. It is not understandable… it's not getting attention"). Write complete plain sentences, never fragments ("Italian on line one." → heard as "online"). Avoid word junctions that make another word ("on line" → "online", "nodded anyway" → "knotted"); prefer idioms people already know ("smiled and nodded"). Say who a new character is in the same sentence ("your colleague Marco"). The hook is the viewer's own moment ("Your client just switched to Italian. You smiled and nodded."), not a setup line ("Same client call. Two ways to survive it.").
10. **Before the founder listens:** tighten silences only (`scripts/voice_tighten.py`, gaps ≤ 0.25 s, no speed-up), put the chosen music bed under the voice, then run the cold-listener test (`scripts/listen_test.py`: Gemini hears it with no script and writes exactly what it heard, possible mishearings, a retell, and clarity/energy/funny scores). On the v2 take it reproduced the founder's "online" mishearing exactly. Ignore its taste notes when they contradict the founder (it suggested a slow deadpan read).

## Directed Gemini recipe (2026-10-02, hook v3; founder: "very AI… no emotion")
**Root cause of "reads the style aloud":** `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` is not driven by a text prefix. Google's docs (ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation) say its text is a *verbatim transcript*; delivery goes in a separate `speech_metadata` annotation on the **Interactions API** (`POST /v1beta/interactions`), not `generateContent`. Our old `tts_gemini.py` sent a prefix through `generateContent`, so it was read aloud. Use `scripts/tts_gemini_directed.py`, which does both formats.
1. **gemini-3.8-flash-tts (winner).** Text input = transcript with inline tags `<short pause> <long pause> <breath> <chuckle> <laugh> <sigh> <gasp> ...` (angle brackets, documented list). Put sustained delivery in a short `style` (STYLE: line of the direction file): persona + mood per line in one sentence, "speaking fairly rapidly with big pitch movement". Keep it concise; the docs warn long "Audio Profile / Director's Notes" blocks cause voice drift. Voice goes in `generation_config.speech_config`. Output is a 24 kHz WAV. Pace control: punctuation, `<short pause>`, and "speaking rapidly/slowly" in style.
2. **gemini-2.5-flash/pro-preview-tts (fallback).** `generateContent` with a director's-notes prompt: `# AUDIO PROFILE: <voice>` + persona, `## THE SCENE` + situation, `### DIRECTOR'S NOTES` (style, pace, per-line notes), `#### TRANSCRIPT` + text. Works and is not read aloud, but not documented for 2.5 and clearly less human (cold listener rated it AI, 1-4/10 for the plain takes). No inline tags documented for 2.5.
3. Tags that render as real mouth sounds (`<chuckle>`, `<sigh>`) are the biggest humanness gain, but one cold-listener run heard the sigh as a pasted sound effect. Use sparingly: one chuckle or sigh per hook, at a turn, not several.
4. Always run `--check`: the Soniox word diff catches read-aloud direction and dropped words (a chuckle is heard as the word "heh", harmless).
5. Pace stays about 2.0-2.5 words/s on a 41-word hook whatever the direction says; measure, then fix with the script and pauses, not speed-up.
7. **Founder pick 2026-10-02: Puck, style + chuckle/sigh tags** (`FINALS/model-race/recipe/voice_reference/puck_hook_founder_pick.mp3`, directions in `marketing/video/FINALS/model-race/recipe/story/voice/v3/`). Reuse that direction file as the starting point for the next film.
6. Pro TTS 429s after about 1 take/day on this key; do experiments on flash and 3.8.

## Rules from runs (moved from SKILL.md lessons 2026-11-06; stories in LESSONS_LOG.md)
- Check every take against the script with Soniox before using it: dropped sentences and read-aloud style prompts hide behind a normal-looking pace (2026-10-02).
- Check auxiliary verbs (has/is, had/is) and word junctions in every take; fix junctions in the script, not the take (2026-10-03).
- `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` needs the Interactions API with `speech_metadata` + inline tags; a text prefix gets read aloud. Use `motion-director/scripts/tts_gemini_directed.py` (2026-10-02).
- Gemini TTS hits a daily 429 after ~6–15 calls: record the founder's top voices first and record chapters in sequence, not in parallel (2026-10-02).
- Even hook takes go through `marketing/skills/voice-modulation/SKILL.md` (2026-10-02).
- Calm a fast take by widening gaps with `voice_edit.py` (run with `lingosync/.venv/bin/python`), never by slowing it; assemble in Python, not zsh (2026-10-02).
- After re-recording a chapter, measure anchor-word onsets from the waveform and correct words.json; keep `words.soniox_raw.json` (2026-10-03).
- Soniox mangles foreign words in an English narration: judge them with the Gemini cold listener, then merge tokens in words.json (2026-10-04).
- Cold listeners don't know post-cutoff model names: judge name clarity by captions and the founder's ear (2026-10-02).
- Gemini with a "natural pace" style gave 128–141 wpm: use the brisk prefix by default (2026-10-02).
- Keep style prompts short; use the founder's chosen hook take as chapter 1; capital `HAS` plus `<short pause>` fixed an auxiliary misread (2026-10-02/03).
- If every take gets the same cold-listener verdict, the script is the problem: fix it before the founder hears takes. Paraphrase jargon quotes in the voice and show them verbatim on screen (2026-10-02).
- Hard foreign names: re-record only the names chapter, with a pronunciation STYLE (2026-10-04).
- Voice cast can carry a story (two-voice "voice war"); write the script in two parts (who says each line) from step 4 and plan hook takes for both voices, but never clone or imitate a real person's voice in a film about voice rights; native-speaker check on accents (2026-10-05).
- Check the fal account isn't locked (`TOP_UP`) before promising ElevenLabs/Brian takes (2026-10-02).
